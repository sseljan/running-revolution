The Running Revolution

How Oregon Changed Fitness

This webpage recreates Douglas Seljan's Oregon History Day trifold project. It includes the images, original quotes, and text from the project. It shows how running moved from a sport for young people to a common hobby and cornerstone of fitness due at least in part to Bill Bowerman and running enthusiasts in Oregon. The project was Dougie's first participation in History Day and received an Honorable Mention for creativity.

Before the Revolution

Before the running revolution, most people did not think of running as something adults did for fun or health. Almost no one ran after high school or college, and people who did run on the road were sometimes laughed at or bothered.

People often went out of their way to mock runners. Drivers would slow down and honk their horns. "Get a horse!" they'd yell, throwing a beer can or soda at the runner's head.

Phil Knight, Shoe Dog

A Short Biography of Bill Bowerman

The Book that Got America Running

Bowerman's 1967 book Jogging helped beginners believe they could try running too. The workouts were simple on purpose. Bowerman was not just writing for athletes; he wanted regular people to get outside and start moving.

That silly book about jogging ... sold a million copies, sparked a movement, changed the very meaning of the word "running."

Phil Knight, Shoe Dog

Nike Capitalizes on the Running Boom

As more people started running, Nike saw an opportunity. Instead of asking runners to make do with dress shoes or uncomfortable gear, Nike made shoes with better cushioning and traction.

Better shoes made running easier on people's feet. As running grew, Nike grew with it.

Community Racing Takes Off

The Butte to Butte race in Eugene became an early example of running as a community event. It began in the 1970s and grew quickly as more people wanted races that felt open to everyone.

Events like this helped show that races did not have to be only for elite competitors. Families, schools, clubs, and neighborhoods could all take part.

Running Today

Today, running is normal. People run for fitness, for fun, for competition, and for time with friends. Community races happen almost every weekend, and many include holiday themes, youth programs, and family-friendly distances.

What started as a strange activity on the side of the road became one of the most common ways people stay active.
